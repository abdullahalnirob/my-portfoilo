## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: prospective freelance / agency clients.** People with a budget and a
problem — a storefront that needs building, a product that needs an engineer,
a landing page that needs to convert. They arrive from a link, a referral, or a
search, usually on a phone, usually comparing several developers at once. Their
job is to decide within a minute or two whether Abdullah can actually ship the
thing they need, and then to start a conversation.

**Secondary: employers and recruiters.** Hiring managers screening for a
full-time role. They need the resume, the experience figures, and the LinkedIn
profile to be findable without hunting. They are a real audience, but when the
two conflict, the design serves the client.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for **Abdullah Al Nirob**, a web developer working across
the full stack. It exists to convert a stranger's attention into a project
conversation. Success is a started conversation — a contact-form submission or a
direct message from someone with real work to commission. Secondary success is a
recruiter downloading the resume.

## Positioning

Every project shown is live at a real URL, and the portfolio says so out loud:
"Nothing here is a mockup." The work is ordered by what would be shown to a
client first, not chronologically, and each card names its actual stack and
whether it is responsive. The claim a neighboring portfolio cannot truthfully
copy is **verifiability** — a visitor can click through and use every single
thing listed, including the early hand-written HTML/CSS exercises, which are
labeled honestly as such rather than hidden.

The services copy makes the same move in the other direction: the list is
deliberately short (three offerings), framed as "the problems I have solved often
enough to estimate honestly," and closes by offering to say when the answer is
none of them.

## Operating Context

- Visitors overwhelmingly arrive on mobile and scroll once, quickly. The page is
  a single long scroll with anchor navigation; there are no sub-routes.
- Clients evaluate by clicking through to live sites. Outbound links to real,
  working deployments are the core interaction, not a footnote.
- Recruiters evaluate by downloading a PDF and checking LinkedIn.
- The site is the top of the funnel; the actual engagement happens in email or
  DMs afterward. Nothing is transacted on the page.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Current surface** — one route (`app/page.tsx`) composed of: `Navbar`, `Hero`,
`ScrollingHeadline`, `BanglaKrisi`, `About`, `Services`, `Projects`.

**Services offered** (three, and only three):

1. E-commerce development — catalog, cart, checkout, order management, admin.
2. Full-stack web development — schema, API, auth, deployment/CI.
3. Static and dynamic landing pages — conversion layout, CMS, SEO, analytics.

**Project taxonomy** — five categories exist in code (`raw`, `frontend`,
`fullstack`, `client`, `video`); only `raw` and `frontend` currently have
entries, and the filter UI hides empty categories by design. Eleven live
projects are listed.

**Technical constraints:**

- Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS v4.
- Motion stack: GSAP (ScrollTrigger + ScrollSmoother) and `motion/react`, plus
  `three` / `@react-three/fiber` / `@shadergradient/react` available but not yet
  used on the live page.
- `ScrollSmoother` transforms `#smooth-content`, which makes it the containing
  block for `position: fixed` descendants. Fixed overlays must be portalled to
  `<body>` (see `Navbar.tsx`) or they will size to the page, not the viewport.
  Any new fixed/sticky element inherits this constraint.
- Project screenshots are remote-hosted on ImgBB; `next.config.ts` allowlists
  `i.ibb.co` and `i.ibb.co.com`.
- Fonts: **ClashDisplay** (variable, self-hosted at `app/fonts/`) for display,
  **Bricolage Grotesque** (next/font) as the sans default, **Geist Mono** for
  numerals and ticks.

**Undecided / open:**

- The `#contact` section does not exist yet, though six CTAs across the page
  point at it. It is required work, not optional.
- The `#testimonials` section does not exist yet; the navbar links to it.
- `<title>` and `<meta description>` are still the create-next-app defaults
  ("Create Next App" / "Generated by create next app").

## Product Principles

1. **Everything shown is clickable and live.** The portfolio's whole argument is
   verifiability. Never introduce a mockup, a dummy card, a "coming soon", or a
   fabricated screenshot — an honest gap beats a filled one.
2. **Label the work honestly, including the weak parts.** Early hand-written
   HTML exercises stay in the list, tagged `Raw code` and `Desktop first`,
   because the range and the honesty are the point.
3. **The client's decision outranks the developer's showmanship.** Motion,
   shaders, and scroll effects earn their place only when they help someone
   decide to hire; they are never the reason a visitor has to wait or scroll.
4. **Say less, and say it exactly.** Three services, not eight. Named stacks,
   not adjectives. Every claim on the page is one Abdullah can back.
5. **Stay legibly Bangladeshi.** The regional identity is an asset and a
   differentiator, not something to neutralize for an international audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Motion is heavy and scroll-driven throughout. `prefers-reduced-motion` must be
  honored everywhere — `ScrollingHeadline` already does this correctly (it
  swaps the hijacked horizontal scroll for a natively scrollable region); the
  GSAP reveals in `About`, `Services`, and `BanglaKrisi` currently do not.
- Content is bilingual in places (Bengali script inside otherwise-English
  copy). Bengali strings need correct `lang` attribution and a font stack that
  actually renders them.
- Mobile-first is a real requirement, not a checkbox: the primary audience
  arrives on a phone.
